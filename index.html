1 Foundations & the law

Why the I-9 exists, who enforces it, and where People Services fits.

The legal basis

The Immigration Reform and Control Act of 1986 (IRCA) amended the Immigration and Nationality Act (INA §274A) to make it unlawful to knowingly hire or continue to employ a person not authorized to work in the U.S. To enforce this, every U.S. employer must verify the identity and employment authorization of each person hired after November 6, 1986, by completing Form I-9, Employment Eligibility Verification.

The agencies you'll hear about

§ The three legal duties you must balance

1) Verify work authorization (don't employ the unauthorized). 2) Don't over-verify or discriminate (don't demand specific or extra documents based on how someone looks or sounds). 3) Don't under-verify (don't skip the form or accept obviously invalid documents). Most violations come from leaning too far one way. Your training is largely about staying in the middle.

Who must have an I-9 — and who's excluded

  • Required: every employee hired to work in the U.S. for wages or other remuneration, regardless of citizenship.
  • Not required: independent contractors and their employees; certain casual domestic workers; people doing sporadic/irregular work; employees hired on or before Nov 6, 1986 in continuous employment; and individuals not physically working in the U.S.
⚠ Never use I-9 status to "pre-screen"

You may not ask for I-9 documents or run E-Verify before a person has accepted a job offer and (for E-Verify) actually started. Verification belongs to onboarding, not recruiting.

Knowledge check

A hiring manager wants to confirm a finalist "can legally work here" before extending an offer and asks you to run E-Verify. What's correct?

Answer: B. Pre-screening with E-Verify or demanding documents before hire is prohibited and is a common discrimination finding. Verification is part of onboarding after the person is hired and (for E-Verify) has started work.

2 The Form I-9 — anatomy & current edition

Know the form before you process it.

🗓 Current edition & the electronic-system deadline

The current Form I-9 is edition 01/20/2025, valid through 05/31/2027. The 08/01/2023 edition is also still acceptable through 05/31/2027. Electronic I-9 systems (including Workday) must be updated to the version showing the 05/31/2027 expiration date by 07/31/2026. The visible change in the 01/20/2025 edition: List B's descriptor changed from "gender" to "sex," plus updated Section 1 status wording. Always confirm the live edition at uscis.gov/i-9.

The four parts of the form

PartWho completes itPurpose
Section 1 — Employee Information & AttestationThe employee (the new hire)Identity info + attestation of citizenship/immigration status, signed under penalty of perjury.
Section 2 — Employer Review & VerificationYou (People Services)Record the documents the employee presents and certify you examined them.
Supplement A — Preparer &/or Translator CertificationAnyone who helped the employee fill out Section 1Used only when someone assists or translates for the employee.
Supplement B — Reverification & RehireYou (People Services)Reverify expiring work authorization, or record a rehire. (Replaces the old "Section 3.")

The modern form merges Sections 1 & 2 onto a single page and moves preparer/translator and reverification into separate Supplements. If you ever see "Section 3," you're looking at an obsolete edition.

Paper vs. electronic (Workday)

An electronically completed and stored I-9 is fully valid if the system meets DHS standards for accuracy, integrity, security, and audit trails, and can reproduce legible copies on request. Workday (or your connected I-9 partner) is your system of record. Practical implications:

  • Electronic signatures with timestamp and an audit trail satisfy the signature requirement.
  • The system must retain document images you upload and let you produce them during an audit.
  • You still follow the same deadlines and document rules — the medium changes, the law doesn't.
💻 In Workday — typical flow

New hire completes Section 1 as an onboarding self-service task (often "Complete Form I-9"). It then routes to a People Services queue as a "Complete I-9 / Verify Documents (Section 2)" task. If E-Verify is enabled, a case is generated from the same data after Section 2. Confirm exact task labels in [your tenant].

Knowledge check

A returning hire's record references a "Section 3." What does that tell you?

Answer: C. "Section 3" only exists on retired editions. Today, rehire and reverification live on Supplement B, and preparer/translator on Supplement A.

3 Deadlines & timelines — the master rule

If you remember one thing from this training, remember the clock.

Before the first day (after offer accepted)
Section 1 may be completed any time after acceptance of the job offer. It must never be required before an offer is accepted.
By the end of Day 1 (first day of work for pay)
Section 1 must be complete — signed and dated by the employee no later than the end of their first day of employment.
Within 3 business days of the start date
Section 2 must be complete (you examined documents and signed). The E-Verify case must be created by this same deadline.
On/before the work-authorization expiration date
Reverify in Supplement B when temporary work authorization expires (only for those who require it — see Module 7).
🗓 The "3 business days" detail everyone gets wrong

Section 2 is due within 3 business days of the first day of employment — meaning if someone starts Monday, you have through end of Thursday (the start day + 3). If the job lasts less than 3 business days, Section 2 must be done by the end of the first day. "Business days" exclude weekends and federal holidays.

What counts as the "first day of employment"?

It's the first day the employee works for pay — not the offer date, not orientation if unpaid, not the badge-pickup day. Anchor every deadline to this date. In Workday, this is typically the Hire Date / Start Date on the position; verify it's accurate because every clock keys off it.

⚠ Late is a violation — but never backdate

If Section 2 or an E-Verify case is late, complete it immediately using the actual date you do the work. Never backdate a form or a case. For a late E-Verify case, the system will ask for a reason — give the honest one. A late-but-truthful record is defensible; a falsified date is fraud.

Knowledge check

An employee's first paid day is Tuesday. By when must Section 2 be completed (assume no holidays)?

Answer: B. Count the first day plus three business days: Tue → Wed, Thu, Fri. The clock runs from the start date, not from when documents happen to show up.

4 Section 1 — Employee information & attestation

The employee owns this section. Your job is to make sure it's done correctly and on time — without doing it for them.

What the employee provides

  • Full legal name (and any other names used), address, date of birth.
  • Social Security Number — optional unless the employer uses E-Verify, in which case it is required. (See SSN-pending in Module 15.)
  • Email and phone — optional.
  • Citizenship/immigration status attestation — exactly one of four boxes.
  • Employee signature and date.

The four attestation categories

#StatusExtra info required
1A citizen of the United StatesNone.
2A noncitizen national of the United StatesNone (rare — e.g., American Samoa).
3A lawful permanent residentUSCIS / Alien Number (A-Number).
4A noncitizen authorized to workWork-authorization expiration date (if any) plus one of: A-Number/USCIS Number, Form I-94 admission number, or foreign passport number + country.
⚠ You may guide, but you may not choose

You can point an employee to instructions and answer process questions, but you must not select their status, tell them which documents to bring, or fill in attestation fields for them. If you actively help complete or translate Section 1, that assistance must be recorded in Supplement A.

Common Section 1 errors to catch before you sign Section 2

  • No box checked, or more than one box checked.
  • Status #3 or #4 selected but the required number/expiration left blank.
  • Missing employee signature or date; date doesn't match the actual day signed.
  • Section 1 dated after the first day of work (late) — note the real dates; don't paper over it.
  • Someone assisted but Supplement A wasn't completed.
💻 In Workday

Section 1 is usually a new-hire onboarding task the employee completes via self-service before or on Day 1. People Services can monitor completion from the onboarding dashboard and nudge stragglers. You generally cannot edit the employee's Section 1 entries — if something's wrong, the employee corrects it (or you use the documented correction path). Confirm the task name and correction routing in [your tenant].

Knowledge check

A new hire selects "A lawful permanent resident" but leaves the number blank, and a colleague translated the form for them. What must be true before you complete Section 2?

Answer: A. Status #3 requires the A-Number/USCIS Number, and any translator/preparer assistance must be recorded in Supplement A. You don't complete the employee's attestation fields for them.

5 Acceptable documents — Lists A, B & C

The employee chooses what to present from the Lists of Acceptable Documents. You verify it fits the rules — you never dictate the choice.

§ The core rule

The employee presents either ONE document from List A (proves identity and work authorization) OR a combination of ONE document from List B (identity) plus ONE from List C (work authorization). That's it: A — or — B + C.

LIST AIdentity + Work Auth
  • U.S. Passport or Passport Card
  • Permanent Resident Card (Form I-551, "green card")
  • Foreign passport with a temporary I-551 stamp/MRIV
  • Employment Authorization Document with photo (Form I-766, "EAD")
  • For specific-employer-authorized workers: foreign passport + Form I-94 with endorsement
  • FSM/RMI passport + Form I-94 (Compact of Free Association)
Present one = done.
LIST BIdentity only
  • State driver's license or ID card with photo/info
  • Federal/state/local government ID card
  • School ID with photo
  • Voter registration card
  • U.S. military card or draft record; military dependent ID
  • USCG Merchant Mariner card; Native American tribal document
  • Canadian driver's license
  • Minors under 18: school/clinic/daycare record
Pair with a List C document.
LIST CWork Auth only
  • Unrestricted Social Security card*
  • Birth certificate (original/certified, U.S.)
  • Certification of Birth Abroad (FS-545, DS-1350, FS-240)
  • Native American tribal document
  • U.S. Citizen ID Card (Form I-197)
  • Resident Citizen ID Card (Form I-179)
  • DHS-issued employment authorization document
*Not valid if marked "not valid for employment" / "valid for work only with DHS authorization."

Rules that apply to every document

  • Unexpired. Expired documents are not acceptable (narrow auto-extension exceptions exist — see Module 15).
  • Original. Photocopies are not acceptable — the one exception is a certified copy of a birth certificate.
  • Genuine & relates to the person. It must reasonably appear authentic and to belong to the employee presenting it. You are not a forensic examiner — apply a reasonable-person standard.
  • List B must have a photograph if the employer participates in E-Verify (so photo matching can work) — confirm your policy.
⚠ Document abuse — the most common discrimination trap

You may not: demand a specific document (e.g., "I need to see your green card"); ask for more documents than the rules require; reject a valid, genuine-looking document; or treat people differently based on citizenship status or national origin. The employee decides which acceptable document(s) to present. Your only question is "does this satisfy A, or B+C?"

Receipts (temporary stand-ins)

A "receipt" can be accepted in limited cases:

  • Receipt for a lost/stolen/damaged document — valid for 90 days; then the employee must present the actual replacement document.
  • Arrival portion of Form I-94 with a temporary I-551 stamp — acts as a List A receipt; present the actual green card when received.
  • Refugee's Form I-94 with refugee admission stamp — receipt for List A; within 90 days present an EAD or a List B doc + unrestricted SS card.

A receipt is never acceptable if the person's actual employment will last fewer than 3 business days.

Deep dive: minors, special placements & "reasonably genuine"

Minors under 18 who cannot present a List B identity document may use a school record/report card, a clinic/doctor/hospital record, or a daycare/nursery record, paired with a List C document. The parent/guardian completes the preparer area as needed.

Certain people with disabilities (in specified placement programs) have a parallel special rule for the identity document.

"Reasonably appears genuine" means a reasonable person would accept it. If a document is obviously altered, doesn't match the person, or is clearly not on the Lists, do not accept it — but ask for clarification respectfully and let the employee present an alternative acceptable document. When in doubt, escalate to your lead/Legal before rejecting.

Knowledge check

An employee offers a state driver's license and an unrestricted Social Security card. You'd prefer a passport "to keep it simple." What do you do?

Answer: C. A valid List B + List C combination fully satisfies the requirement. Demanding a specific document (the passport) or extra documents is document abuse.

6 Section 2 — Employer review & verification

This is your core task. Examine the documents, record them accurately, and certify — within 3 business days.

Step by step

  1. Confirm Section 1 is complete and signed (and timely). Don't proceed on an incomplete Section 1.
  2. Examine the original document(s) the employee presents — physically in person, or via the remote alternative procedure if you qualify (below).
  3. Check: unexpired, original, reasonably genuine, relates to this person, and satisfies A or B+C.
  4. Record each document's title, issuing authority, document number, and expiration date in the correct List column. Don't mix a List A entry with B/C entries.
  5. Enter the employee's first day of employment (start date) in the certification.
  6. Sign & date the employer certification (your name, title, and the business name/address).
  7. If using the remote procedure, check the alternative-procedure box and retain copies.
§ Examine the real thing — physically or by the approved remote procedure

The default is physical, in-person examination of original documents. As a permanent option, E-Verify employers in good standing may use the DHS remote alternative procedure: examine front/back copies, conduct a live video interaction with the employee holding the documents, retain clear copies, and check the alternative-procedure box on the I-9. If you offer it at a site, offer it consistently (don't pick and choose by person).

🗓 Three business days — from the start date

Section 2 must be signed within 3 business days of the first day of employment. Same deadline as opening the E-Verify case. Don't wait for "a batch" — process each new hire on its own clock.

The "Additional Information" box

Use it to note things that affect the record: receipt details and follow-up dates, EAD auto-extension facts, the remote-examination method, termination dates, etc. Keep notes factual and dated.

Common Section 2 mistakes

  • Recording a document in the wrong List column (e.g., putting a List A item under B).
  • Filling in both a List A entry and B+C entries (you pick one path, not both).
  • Missing issuing authority, number, or expiration date.
  • Missing the employer signature, title, or date.
  • Wrong first-day-of-employment date in the certification.
  • Accepting an expired or photocopied document.
💻 In Workday

Open the "Verify I-9 / Complete Form I-9 (Section 2)" task from your inbox. Workday pre-fills the employee's name and start date; you select the document(s), enter title/authority/number/expiration, and upload images. Many tenants require document images and validate that you complete either List A or List B+C (not both). Submitting Section 2 typically triggers the E-Verify case (Module 9). Confirm whether image upload is mandatory and how remote-procedure is flagged in [your tenant].

Knowledge check

You're completing Section 2 and the employee presented a U.S. Passport (List A). How should you record it?

Answer: B. One List A document stands alone — complete the List A fields only. Don't also fill List B/C, and don't ask for extra documents.

7 Supplement A (preparer/translator) & Supplement B (reverification & rehire)

Two supplements handle help with the form and the life-cycle events that come after onboarding.

Supplement A — Preparer and/or Translator Certification

  • Used when anyone other than the employee helps fill out or translate Section 1.
  • The helper records their name/address and signs. A separate area exists for multiple preparers/translators across the form's life.
  • If no one assisted, the employee checks that box and Supplement A is not used.

Supplement B — Reverification & Rehire

This replaces the old Section 3. You (People Services) complete it for two situations:

A) Reverification of expiring work authorization

§ Who gets reverified — and who never does

Reverify only employees whose employment authorization or the document proving it has an expiration date (e.g., many EAD holders). Do NOT reverify: U.S. citizens and noncitizen nationals; lawful permanent residents who showed a green card (even when the card itself expires); or List B identity documents (an expired driver's license is not a reverification trigger).

  • When: on or before the work-authorization expiration date — never let it lapse.
  • What: the employee presents an unexpired List A or List C document of their choice (you don't dictate which). Record it in Supplement B and sign.
  • Tickler: track expiration dates proactively; start the process well ahead (e.g., 90 days out).

B) Rehire

  • If you rehire someone within 3 years of the original I-9 date, you may complete Supplement B on the existing I-9 instead of a new form — if the original is still valid and on the current rules.
  • If their authorization has since changed/expired, reverify at the same time.
  • Many organizations simply complete a new I-9 for rehires to stay clean — follow your policy.

C) Name changes

You may record a legal name change in Supplement B. There's no requirement to see documentation of a name change, though noting the basis is good practice.

💻 In Workday

Expiring-authorization reverification tasks are often auto-generated from the work-auth expiration date captured at hire and surface in a People Services queue ahead of expiration. Rehires may launch a Supplement B or a fresh I-9 depending on configuration. Confirm how your tenant triggers reverification ticklers and whether rehire creates a new I-9 in [your tenant].

Knowledge check

A lawful permanent resident's green card is about to expire. The original I-9 used that card for List A. Do you reverify?

Answer: C. Permanent residents' work authorization doesn't lapse with the card. Reverifying an LPR (or demanding a renewed card) is a classic over-verification/document-abuse error.

8 E-Verify fundamentals

E-Verify is an electronic confirmation layer on top of the I-9 — not a replacement for it.

What it is

E-Verify is a free, web-based system run by USCIS (DHS) in partnership with the SSA. It compares the information from an employee's Form I-9 against DHS and SSA records to confirm employment eligibility. You still complete the I-9; E-Verify checks it.

Who must use it

  • Voluntary for most private employers — but once enrolled, you must follow the MOU and use it for all new hires at participating sites (no cherry-picking).
  • Mandatory for many federal contractors/subcontractors with the FAR E-Verify clause, and under various state laws.
§ The rules of use (memorize these)
  • No pre-screening: create a case only after hire and start, never for applicants.
  • No selective verification: run every new hire at a participating site, not just some.
  • No adverse action while a case is open/contested: you may not fire, suspend, dock pay, delay start/training, or otherwise penalize someone because of a pending case or a mismatch they're contesting.
  • Generally don't run existing employees (FAR contractors are an exception per their clause).
  • Post the notices: display the E-Verify Participation and the IER "Right to Work" posters (English & Spanish) where applicants/employees can see them.

Enrollment & the MOU

Participation requires enrollment and signing a Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) that binds the employer to E-Verify's rules. Designated, trained users create and manage cases. Your organization is already enrolled — your job is correct day-to-day use.

Photo matching

When an employee presents a U.S. Passport/Passport Card, Permanent Resident Card (I-551), or EAD (I-766), E-Verify displays a photo from government records. You must compare it to the photo on the document the employee gave you and confirm whether they match. This is why a photo is needed on documents for E-Verify employers.

Knowledge check

Your company is enrolled in E-Verify. A manager asks you to "only run E-Verify on people we're unsure about." Correct response?

Answer: A. Selective verification — including running only non-citizens — violates the MOU and anti-discrimination law. Enrolled sites verify every new hire.

9 Creating & managing E-Verify cases in Workday

Where the I-9 data becomes a verified case. The exact screens depend on whether your tenant uses native Workday E-Verify or a connected partner.

💻 Two common configurations

(a) Native Workday E-Verify — completing Section 2 generates the case inside Workday; results and follow-up tasks appear in your Workday inbox. (b) Connected partner (Tracker I-9, Equifax/Guardian, WorkBright, etc.) — Workday passes data to the partner, where case actions happen, sometimes via single sign-on. Identify which one you use and substitute the exact task names below. Document the path for [your tenant].

When to create the case

🗓 Within 3 business days of the start date

Create the E-Verify case no later than 3 business days after the employee's first day of employment — the same deadline as Section 2. If you're late, the system will require a reason; choose the accurate one (e.g., "Other" with a brief honest explanation). Never falsify dates.

Step by step (generalized)

  1. Complete & submit Section 2 — this is what feeds the case. Verify name, DOB, SSN, citizenship status, and document data are correct; typos here cause mismatches.
  2. Generate the case from the I-9 data (automatic on Section 2 submit in many tenants, or via a "Create E-Verify Case" action).
  3. Handle photo matching if prompted (Passport/Passport Card, I-551, or I-766): compare the displayed photo to the document and answer match / no-match / no-photo-displayed.
  4. Resolve duplicate-case alerts — if E-Verify flags an existing case for the same person, review and choose the correct reason to continue (don't blindly duplicate).
  5. Record the result and act on it (Module 10).

SSN is required for E-Verify

Because E-Verify checks SSA records, the SSN field on the I-9 is mandatory for E-Verify employers. If a new hire has applied for but not yet received an SSN, see the SSN-pending process in Module 15 — you complete the I-9 and delay the case until the SSN arrives, noting the reason.

Case alerts & dashboards

E-Verify (and Workday/partner dashboards) surface action items: cases awaiting referral, mismatches needing notification, cases to close, photo-match steps. Work these queues daily — the legal clocks (Module 10) run whether or not you've looked.

⚠ Garbage in, mismatch out

A huge share of mismatches are data-entry errors: transposed SSN digits, wrong DOB, name mismatches (hyphenation, two last names, suffixes), or selecting the wrong citizenship status. Double-check these against the actual documents before submitting the case. It saves the employee a stressful mismatch and saves you the resolution work.

Knowledge check

It's day 4 after a new hire's start date and you realize the E-Verify case was never created. What do you do?

Answer: B. Always create the case as soon as you can, with truthful dates and the required late reason. Backdating is fraud; skipping abandons your obligation.

10 E-Verify results & the mismatch (TNC) process

Most cases resolve instantly. The ones that don't have strict, employee-protective procedures — this is the highest-stakes part of your role.

The possible results

ResultMeaningYour action
Employment AuthorizedData matched DHS/SSA records.Close the case (often automatic). Done.
Verification in ProcessReferred to DHS/SSA for manual review.Wait for an update; no action yet.
Tentative Nonconfirmation (Mismatch)Info didn't match DHS and/or SSA records — not a finding that the person can't work.Run the mismatch process below — promptly.
Case in ContinuanceEmployee contacted the agency; more time needed.Keep the person working; wait. No adverse action.
Close Case and ResubmitA data error needs correcting.Close, fix the data, create a new case.
Final NonconfirmationEligibility could not be confirmed after the process.You may terminate; must close the case recording the outcome.
DHS/SSA No Action / No ShowEmployee didn't take required action in time.Close; may terminate per the guidance.
⚠ A mismatch is NOT proof of anything

A Tentative Nonconfirmation (mismatch) only means the data didn't match — often due to a typo, a name change not reported to SSA, or records out of sync. You may not terminate, suspend, reduce hours/pay, delay start/training, or tell the employee they "failed." The employee has the right to contest and keep working while they do.

The mismatch resolution timeline

Within 10 federal working days of the mismatch — EMPLOYER
Notify the employee privately as soon as possible; give them a copy of the Further Action Notice (FAN); review it together and have them confirm whether the info at the top is correct; the employee decides to take action (contest) or not, and you both sign. If they choose to take action, you refer the case in E-Verify and give them the Referral Date Confirmation.
If not contested or no decision by day 10
If the employee declines to take action, or gives no decision by the end of the 10th federal working day, you close the case; E-Verify cannot confirm work authorization and employment may be terminated.
After referral — EMPLOYEE has 8 federal working days
Once you refer the case, the employee has 8 federal working days to visit SSA (SSA mismatch) or call DHS (DHS mismatch) to resolve it. They keep working throughout.
Resolution
E-Verify updates to Employment Authorized, Case in Continuance (more time), or Final Nonconfirmation. Act per the result; always close the case with the correct closure reason.
§ "Federal working days"

Both clocks count federal government working days — Monday–Friday excluding federal holidays. Don't count weekends or holidays. The 10-day employer clock starts when E-Verify issues the mismatch; the 8-day employee clock starts at referral.

How to deliver a mismatch the right way

✓ Do

  • Tell the employee privately and promptly.
  • Explain it's a data mismatch, not an accusation, and that they may contest.
  • Give the FAN in a language/format they can understand; offer it in Spanish if appropriate.
  • Keep them working and paid normally throughout.
  • Document each step and date in the case/notes.

✕ Don't

  • Announce it in front of coworkers.
  • Tell them to "fix their papers or you're gone."
  • Suspend, cut hours, withhold pay, or delay training.
  • Refer the case without their decision and signature.
  • Let either clock lapse silently.
Deep dive: closing cases & choosing a closure reason

Every E-Verify case must eventually be closed with an accurate reason — e.g., employment authorized; the employee was terminated for a reason unrelated to E-Verify; the case was created in error; the employee did not contest; or final nonconfirmation. The closure reason is part of your audit trail. If you created a duplicate or made a data error, use Close Case and Resubmit and open a corrected case rather than editing around it.

If you terminate after a Final Nonconfirmation, record that the termination was based on the FNC and close accordingly. If you choose to keep employing someone after an FNC, E-Verify requires you to notify DHS — follow the prompt; this is unusual and should involve Legal.

Scenario

Maria starts Monday. You open her case Tuesday; it returns an SSA mismatch. You realize she recently married and changed her name with you but maybe not with SSA. What's the right sequence?

Show the recommended handling
  1. First, verify your data entry against her documents. If you mistyped her name/SSN, use Close Case and Resubmit with corrected data — that may clear it with no mismatch at all.
  2. If your data is correct, treat it as a genuine mismatch: notify Maria privately, give and review the FAN, and let her decide.
  3. If she chooses to take action, refer the case and give her the Referral Date Confirmation. She has 8 federal working days to visit SSA (likely to update her name).
  4. She keeps working and is paid normally. You take no adverse action and wait for E-Verify to update.
Knowledge check

An employee receives a mismatch and chooses to contest it. While the case is open, the employee may be:

Answer: C. While a mismatch is being contested, the employee continues working under normal terms. Suspension, reduced hours, or withholding pay are prohibited adverse actions.

11 Anti-discrimination & employee rights (DOJ–IER)

The same law that requires verification forbids using it to discriminate. People Services sits exactly on this line.

What the INA prohibits

  • Citizenship-status discrimination — treating people differently in hiring, firing, or verification because they are/aren't citizens, permanent residents, refugees, asylees, etc.
  • National-origin discrimination — based on country of origin, accent, name, or appearance.
  • Unfair documentary practices ("document abuse") — demanding specific documents, more than required, or rejecting valid ones, based on a person's status or origin.
  • Retaliation — punishing someone for asserting their rights or filing a complaint.

✓ Do

  • Let the employee choose which acceptable document(s) to present.
  • Apply identical procedures to every new hire.
  • Accept any document that's on the Lists, unexpired, and reasonably genuine.
  • Offer the remote procedure consistently if you offer it at all.

✕ Don't

  • Say "I need your green card / passport specifically."
  • Ask non-citizens for "extra proof."
  • Re-verify people who don't require it (citizens, LPRs).
  • Run E-Verify only on certain groups.
  • Set work-authorization expiration as a hiring filter.
§ "Future expiration date" is not a hiring factor

You may not refuse to hire, or treat differently, someone whose work authorization has a future expiration date. A temporary EAD is just as valid for I-9 purposes as a passport on the day it's presented. Reverify later if required — don't discriminate now.

Knowledge check

Two new hires present valid documents. One has an EAD expiring in 14 months; the other a U.S. passport. A manager says "let's not bother with the EAD person — too much hassle later." Your response?

Answer: A. A future expiration date can't drive an employment decision, and asking for extra documents is document abuse. Reverify later only if/when required.

12 Retention, storage & government audits

Completing the I-9 is half the job; keeping it correctly and producing it on demand is the other half.

The retention formula

§ Keep each I-9 for the LATER of:

3 years after the date of hire  —or—  1 year after the date employment ends, whichever is later. Compute the date when someone leaves and purge only when both conditions are satisfied.

Quick method at termination: (a) hire date + 3 years; (b) termination date + 1 year; keep until the later date.

Storage best practices

  • Store I-9s separately from general personnel files (so an auditor sees only I-9s, not unrelated records).
  • If you keep copies of documents, do it consistently for everyone — copying for some and not others can look discriminatory.
  • Electronic storage (Workday/partner) must protect integrity, prevent unauthorized changes, keep an audit trail, and reproduce legible copies on request.
  • Maintain a purge schedule so you don't keep records past their retention requirement.

Government inspections

🎯 Self-audit periodically

Run internal I-9 audits to find and correct errors before the government does. Do it consistently and neutrally (not targeting individuals), correct properly (Module 13), and document your methodology. A clean, well-documented self-audit program is strong evidence of good faith.

💻 In Workday

Use I-9 reporting to monitor missing/incomplete forms, upcoming reverifications, open E-Verify cases, and retention/purge dates. Confirm your tenant can export the I-9 and uploaded document images for an auditor on short notice. Identify the standard report names in [your tenant].

Knowledge check

An employee hired on Jan 1, 2022 leaves on Jan 1, 2026. When can their I-9 be purged?

Answer: B. Hire + 3 years = Jan 1, 2025; termination + 1 year = Jan 1, 2027. Keep until the later date — Jan 1, 2027.

13 Corrections & the most common errors

Mistakes happen. How you correct them matters as much as the original entry.

Golden rules of correcting a paper I-9

  • Never erase, white-out, or obscure the original entry.
  • Draw a single line through the error, enter the correct information, and add your initials and the date of the correction.
  • Section 1 errors should be corrected by the employee; Section 2 errors by the employer. Don't fix the other party's section.
  • If a form is missing, complete a new one now using the current date — don't backdate. Attach a brief signed memo explaining why it's late.
  • Don't create a brand-new form to hide an error on the old one; correct transparently and keep both if a new form is warranted.
💻 In Workday / electronic systems

Electronic I-9s are corrected through the system's amendment/correction workflow, which preserves the prior values in an audit trail (the electronic equivalent of "line-through-initial-date"). Don't try to overwrite history. For employee-side (Section 1) corrections, route the task back to the employee. Confirm the correction/amendment path in [your tenant].

14 Penalties & enforcement

Why precision pays off — the cost of getting it wrong, at scale.

⚠ Penalty amounts change every year

Federal civil penalties are adjusted for inflation annually. Treat the figures below as orders of magnitude only and confirm current amounts (8 CFR 274a.10 and the DHS Federal Register adjustments) before quoting anyone.

ViolationNatureApprox. exposure (per violation/worker — verify current)
Paperwork / substantive I-9 violationsCivilHundreds to a few thousand dollars per form — multiplied across a workforce, this adds up fast.
Knowingly hiring / continuing to employ unauthorized workersCivil (escalates by offense #)Roughly hundreds to tens of thousands of dollars per worker, rising sharply for repeat offenses.
Unfair documentary practices / discrimination (IER)Civil + back payPer-individual penalties plus possible back pay and corrective orders.
Pattern or practice / fraudCriminalFines and potential imprisonment; debarment from federal contracts.

Beyond fines

  • Debarment from federal contracts for serious violations.
  • E-Verify MOU termination for misuse (e.g., pre-screening, selective verification, adverse action during mismatches).
  • Reputational harm and operational disruption from a worksite enforcement action.
🎯 "Good faith" is a real defense

For many technical/procedural paperwork errors, a documented good-faith compliance program, timely corrections, and consistent self-audits can reduce or avoid penalties. Your careful, consistent processing is the company's defense.

Knowledge check

Which best reflects how I-9 paperwork penalties scale?

Answer: B. Civil penalties are assessed per form/worker and are inflation-adjusted each year, so consistent accuracy across volume is what protects the organization.

15 Special situations you'll actually encounter

The edge cases that generate most of your questions. Confirm specifics with Legal where noted.

Remote & hybrid hires (the alternative procedure)

If your organization is an E-Verify employer in good standing, you may verify documents remotely: have the employee send front/back copies, conduct a live video interaction while they hold the originals, confirm the documents reasonably appear genuine and relate to them, check the alternative-procedure box on the I-9, and retain the copies. Create the E-Verify case as usual. Offer the procedure consistently at a site (don't single people out). If you're not an E-Verify employer in good standing, you must do a physical, in-person exam (an authorized representative may do it on your behalf — that representative acts for the employer, so train them).

New hire has applied for but not received an SSN

Complete the I-9 (Section 1 can note the SSN is pending). Because E-Verify needs the SSN, delay creating the case until the SSN arrives, then create it and record the reason for the delay. Don't withhold employment or treat the person adversely for a pending SSN.

F-1 students: OPT, STEM OPT, CPT

Typically present an EAD (I-766) for OPT (List A), or for CPT a combination tied to the I-20/I-94. STEM OPT has specific extension rules. Track the work-auth expiration for reverification. The details are nuanced — when unsure, confirm with your immigration counsel rather than guessing.

EAD auto-extensions & the 180-day rule

Certain EAD categories are automatically extended when a timely renewal is filed — historically up to 180 days, with longer temporary extensions announced at times. The combination of the expiring EAD plus the receipt notice can establish continued authorization for the extension window. Note the facts and the auto-extension end date in the Additional Information box and reverify at the right time. Verify the current auto-extension length in USCIS guidance — it has changed.

TPS (Temporary Protected Status)

TPS beneficiaries often have EADs that may be auto-extended by Federal Register notice. Follow the specific notice for the country/designation in effect and document the extension basis. Confirm against the current notice — these change with each redesignation/extension.

Rehires & name changes

Rehire within 3 years: you may use Supplement B on the prior I-9 (if still valid and on current rules), reverifying if authorization changed. Many orgs prefer a fresh I-9 — follow policy. Name change: record in Supplement B; documentation isn't required but noting the basis is good practice.

Lawful permanent residents (never reverify)

An LPR who presented a green card for List A is not reverified, even when the card expires. The card expiring doesn't end their work authorization. Asking for a renewed card is over-verification.

Mergers, acquisitions & authorized representatives

In an M&A, the successor can either treat acquired staff as new hires (new I-9s) or adopt the predecessor's I-9s (inheriting any defects) — a decision for Legal. An authorized representative (e.g., a notary, manager, or third party) may complete Section 2 on the employer's behalf for remote in-person exams; the employer remains liable for their errors, so provide instructions.

Knowledge check

A fully remote new hire can't come to an office. Your company is an E-Verify employer in good standing. What's the cleanest compliant path?

Answer: A. The live video interaction, retained copies, and checking the alternative-procedure box are required elements — and you must offer it consistently. Photos alone (no video) don't satisfy the procedure.
